# ADR-005: 0.2B Replacement-Evidence Protocol

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-08
**Prototype:** 0.2B
**Decision:** D75

## Context

Milestone 0.2B's component lifecycle mechanism, versioned records, replay,
reports, benchmark support and qualification registry are implemented, but the
qualification/balance acceptance milestone remains open.

- C1 and C2 are immutable historical qualification configurations. C2
  (`component-impact-c2`, checksum `13548462df34a183`) remains the unchanged
  experimental runtime default; it is not an accepted final balance solution.
- AB2 (`component-impact-ab2`, checksum `6b9f70450d3f10b8`) passed its
  development lifecycle suite, but its separately authorised one-time
  confirmation failed the strict representative-light terminal gate at exactly
  `0.85`. D32 permanently sealed that result and made AB2 ineligible for
  default promotion.
- The original `prototype-0.2-baseline-v1` held-out partition is spent. The
  current lifecycle runner rejects `held-out` and `all` before seed selection or
  simulation, and no later candidate may be validated against that partition.

ADR-003 remains the historical authority for the original seed bank and its
original held-out policy. D24-D32 remain the historical record of the 0.2B
candidates, gates and AB2 result. This ADR governs only a future replacement
cycle; it does not rewrite or reopen those decisions.

No candidate, fixture, seed partition or numerical threshold is selected by
this ADR. No benchmark or held-out execution is authorised by it.

## Decision summary

0.2B may continue only through four explicitly separated evidence stages:

1. ordinary semantic/unit verification;
2. development-only qualification evidence;
3. whole-combat development acceptance; and
4. one-time confirmation on a genuinely fresh held-out partition.

The stages have different authority. Lifecycle correctness and
component-transition viability are not whole-combat balance or
finish-distribution acceptance. A future candidate must be immutable and
completely identified before fresh held-out confirmation. Replacement
development evidence uses a bounded, versioned multi-fixture strategy rather
than a single fixture. A future held-out partition is generated and held
outside repository-visible candidate-design inputs by an independent
evaluation custodian and is spent regardless of pass or fail.

## 1. Evidence stages

### Stage 1 — ordinary semantic/unit verification

This stage may use ordinary unit fixtures, synthetic inputs and deterministic
non-benchmark regression cases. It verifies, as applicable:

- candidate configuration schema, complete identity and canonical checksum;
- immutable registration and fail-closed unknown-ID/configuration handling;
- qualification formula boundaries and lifecycle transition legality;
- explicit event facts, guard semantics, record/replay version boundaries and
  factual report reconstruction;
- fixture legality, role assignment, provenance and checksum binding; and
- authorization boundaries, including rejection of held-out and `all` before
  any simulation.

This stage may conclude that a contract or implementation behaves according to
its specified semantics. It may not conclude that a candidate is qualified,
balanced, generalizes to unseen seeds, improves an agent, or is suitable for
default or public use.

### Stage 2 — development-only qualification evidence

After the candidate, development fixture suite, development protocol and
qualification gates have been versioned, the authorized development partition
may be used for qualification evidence. The candidate-design process may
inspect development facts under the approved protocol, and failed candidates
must remain identifiable historical records rather than being silently
overwritten.

This stage may support conclusions about deterministic lifecycle behavior,
transition viability, factual completeness, replay compatibility and the
predeclared development qualification gates for the named fixture suite. It
may produce diagnostics for candidate selection within the frozen development
protocol.

It may not support held-out generalization, final 0.2B acceptance, public or
default balance claims, ranking, strongest/best conclusions, or conclusions
about grid or opponent-suite balance. It must not read the spent held-out
partition or any later operational evidence.

### Stage 3 — whole-combat development acceptance

This is a distinct stage from lifecycle qualification. It evaluates the frozen
candidate across the bounded multi-fixture development suite so that
finish-distribution and whole-combat behavior are not mechanically determined
by one high-armour, one low-armour or one diagnostic pairing.

The stage may support only a protocol-specific conclusion that the candidate
passed or failed the predeclared whole-combat development acceptance for the
named fixtures, role assignments, simulator/ruleset/catalogue identities and
development partition. It does not establish held-out generalization, final
balance, public readiness, fairness, a best design or a strongest/weakest
opponent.

The following metric categories must be declared before candidate execution,
with per-fixture and suite aggregation rules where relevant:

- outcome distribution: wins, losses and draws for explicitly named roles;
- finish methods: destruction, immobilisation, judges and draw outcomes;
- first-round finishes and round-duration distribution, including cap
  incidence;
- final integrity and other existing factual combat-state distributions;
- component transition and terminal-disable counts by component;
- terminal-disable mix, component dominance and damaged-state persistence; and
- role-swapped or asymmetric fixture facts needed to describe the protocol's
  coverage, without interpreting them as slot fairness.

Existing D24-D32 evidence supports separating these categories from lifecycle
gates, but it does not authorize a new universal numeric threshold set. The
historical values and diagnostics in ADR-002, D27-D30 and the 0.2B plan remain
historical evidence unless a later protocol decision expressly adopts them.
Any whole-combat threshold, denominator rule, minimum sample rule or
investigation rule must be frozen before candidate execution. It must not be
chosen, relaxed or tightened after seeing development or held-out results.

### Stage 4 — one-time fresh held-out confirmation

Only a candidate that has passed the predeclared development stages and has
been independently reviewed and frozen may enter fresh held-out confirmation.
This stage confirms the already-frozen candidate and gates under a new
partition; it is not a tuning or redesign stage.

The confirmation may support only a factual pass/fail/indeterminate decision
against the frozen protocol, together with the approved aggregate evidence. It
does not promote C2, revive AB2, close 0.2B automatically, authorize a default
runtime, or establish public/ranked/tournament balance.

## 2. Candidate authority and versioning

Every future candidate must satisfy all of the following before any fresh
held-out confirmation:

1. It has a new immutable candidate ID. Reusing C1, C2 or AB2 for changed
   semantics is prohibited.
2. Its complete configuration is recorded, including model, constants,
   schema/version identity, qualification behavior and any other input that
   can affect results.
3. A canonical configuration serialization and checksum bind the complete
   configuration. The candidate record also binds the versioned development
   fixture/protocol contract and its checksum.
4. The candidate, all lifecycle gates, all whole-combat metric categories and
   all acceptance thresholds/denominator rules are frozen before the fresh
   held-out partition is introduced to the evaluator.
5. Any change to an authoritative field creates a new candidate record and
   checksum. No report, replay or registry may silently treat two candidates
   as the same configuration.

C2 is not mutated by this ADR and remains the experimental default unless a
separate accepted decision changes that status. AB2 is frozen for historical
reproducibility and remains permanently ineligible for default promotion. No
candidate constants are selected here.

## 3. Replacement development-fixture strategy

Future development evidence uses a bounded, versioned multi-fixture suite.
The suite is a protocol input, not an outcome-derived tuning artifact.

### Permitted fixture inputs

- Existing canonical build and policy definitions may be reused only as
  explicitly versioned inputs with their identities and checksums recorded.
- Dedicated evaluation fixtures may be introduced by a later fixture/protocol
  decision when a canonical build does not isolate the lifecycle or
  whole-combat question. Such fixtures need their own immutable IDs, complete
  build/policy definitions, purpose, classification, version and checksum.
- A bounded combination of canonical and dedicated fixtures is preferred when
  it gives better coverage without changing simulator semantics.

The replacement suite must cover lifecycle stress and whole-combat behavior
through more than one fixture. Its contract should include, as applicable, a
high-armour/reinforced-drive lifecycle stress case, an unguarded or
no-utility progression case, a representative lower-armour case, and a
role-swapped asymmetric or other diagnostic pairing. These are coverage
principles, not new fixture identities or tuning instructions. A single
fixture may not be the sole acceptance basis when its frozen damage, armour,
policy or match-cap mechanics make a whole-combat gate impossible or
unrepresentative.

Each fixture and suite must bind its simulator, ruleset, catalogue,
qualification, role assignment, policy/build identities, development partition,
classification and canonical checksum. Hard acceptance fixtures and
diagnostic fixtures must be distinguished before execution.

No replacement fixture may be selected or tuned from prior 0.2D outcomes,
grid-beta outcomes, AB2 held-out results, the spent held-out partition,
provider output or any other prohibited evidence. No fixture bytes are created
or changed by this ADR.

## 4. Whole-combat acceptance boundary

The lifecycle stage answers whether component transitions are legal,
observable, replayable and viable under the approved qualification protocol.
The whole-combat stage separately answers whether the frozen candidate meets
the predeclared outcome, finish, duration, integrity and component-distribution
criteria across the bounded development suite.

The following are explicitly not interchangeable:

- non-zero transitions do not prove a useful or balanced finish distribution;
- low terminal-disable incidence does not prove acceptable structural damage,
  duration, judges or draw behavior;
- passing a hard lifecycle fixture does not qualify a candidate across all
  whole-combat fixtures; and
- a diagnostic fixture result cannot be promoted into a hard acceptance claim
  without a new protocol decision.

No strongest/best, weakest/worst, fairness, meta, tier, tuning or ranking
conclusion may be emitted from either development stage. Existing factual
reports may preserve observations, but interpretation requires the accepted
protocol and remains limited to its declared decision.

## 5. Fresh held-out custody and one-time use

The replacement held-out partition must be genuinely unavailable to the
candidate-design AI before candidate freeze. A repository-visible deterministic
master seed, generator input, seed list, or reconstructible generation recipe
must not be used to create the replacement partition before that freeze.

### Custodian and creation

- An independent evaluation custodian, outside the candidate-design AI context
  and outside ordinary repository-controlled development tooling, generates the
  partition using private high-entropy input from a controlled random source.
- The custodian creates the partition only after the candidate, development
  fixture/protocol contract, all gates and the independent freeze record are
  complete. The candidate-design AI must not receive the partition or its
  generation inputs before that point.
- The custodian retains the ordered canonical partition in access-controlled
  custody. It is not committed to the repository, placed in a benchmark
  fixture, or made available through a normal development command.

### Non-revealing identity and commitment

The public decision record may contain a fresh opaque `bankId`, schema and
generation-protocol version, partition counts, simulator/ruleset/catalogue
identities, fixture/protocol checksum and a cryptographic commitment to the
ordered canonical payload. The commitment should be an HMAC-SHA-256 (or a
later accepted equivalent) under a custodian-held secret; a bare hash of a
small seed domain is not sufficient protection against reconstruction. The
secret and all seed values remain outside the repository and candidate-design
AI context.

The record must not contain individual seeds, generation inputs, ordering
contents, per-seed details or any artifact from which the partition can be
reconstructed before confirmation.

### Executor and released result

- An independent confirmation executor receives the frozen candidate bundle
  and the sealed partition through the custodian-controlled channel. It may
  execute only the frozen candidate and protocol; it may not alter constants,
  gates, fixtures or the partition.
- The released result may contain the candidate/configuration and protocol
  checksums, bank ID and commitment, aggregate counts/rates, confidence data
  specified by the protocol, per-gate status and a deterministic result/report
  checksum.
- Individual held-out seeds, seed order, per-seed results and detailed
  per-match held-out records must not be returned to candidate-design AI or
  used to redesign the candidate. Any later human review must preserve the
  same evidence firewall.

The partition is spent immediately after confirmation begins, regardless of
pass, fail, infrastructure error or indeterminate result. It must never be
rerun for another candidate, with changed gates, or after a partial result. A
new candidate cycle requires a new independently generated partition and a new
custodian commitment.

## 6. Relationship to 0.2E

Future 0.2E may use C2 as a **frozen non-final baseline** only if its own
accepted governance decision records:

- the exact C2 ID and checksum (`component-impact-c2` /
  `13548462df34a183`);
- the simulator, ruleset, catalogue, fixture/protocol and report identities;
- that C2 is a comparison baseline, not a 0.2B acceptance, balance approval or
  default-promotion decision; and
- that any redesign changing component-qualification semantics is a new 0.2B
  candidate subject to this ADR before fresh held-out confirmation.

Using C2 in that role does not close 0.2B, make C2 final, permit AB2 promotion,
or authorize adaptation, provider calls, held-out access or 0.2E execution by
itself. Existing 0.2D factual reports and grid-beta outcomes are not new
qualification or balance evidence. 0.2E remains separately governed and is not
started by this ADR.

## 7. Phased sequence

The next 0.2B cycle must follow this sequence:

1. **Governance accepted.** ADR-005 and its decision record are accepted.
2. **Development fixture/protocol contract.** Version the bounded multi-fixture
   strategy, fixture identities/checksums, metric categories, gate definitions,
   denominator rules and any thresholds. No candidate or execution is needed
   to approve this contract.
3. **Candidate selection and registration.** Select no constants in advance of
   that contract; then register one new immutable candidate with complete
   configuration and checksum.
4. **Semantic/unit verification.** Verify the candidate and protocol contracts
   without benchmark or held-out execution.
5. **Development qualification evidence.** Run only the authorized development
   partition and record factual lifecycle evidence and predeclared gates.
6. **Whole-combat development acceptance.** Apply the separately frozen
   whole-combat categories and gates across the bounded suite.
7. **Independent review and freeze.** Verify provenance, candidate identity,
   reports and gates; no changes are permitted after the freeze record.
8. **Externally controlled confirmation.** The independent custodian introduces
   one fresh held-out partition and the executor runs the frozen candidate once.
9. **Final factual decision.** Record pass, fail or indeterminate with the
   released aggregate evidence and spent-partition status. No redesign occurs
   from detailed held-out results.

## Consequences

This ADR preserves C2, AB2, ADR-003 and the closed 0.2D contracts while making
the evidence boundary explicit for a future 0.2B cycle. It prevents lifecycle
correctness from being mistaken for whole-combat acceptance and prevents a
repository-visible seed recipe from being called genuinely held out.

The protocol adds a governance and custody dependency before any candidate can
be evaluated. This is intentional: without a versioned multi-fixture contract,
predeclared whole-combat gates and an independent fresh partition, another
candidate result would not provide an accepted basis for 0.2B closure.

## Next task

Recommend exactly one next task after this ADR:

**Create and accept the versioned 0.2B replacement development-fixture and
protocol contract.** This is documentation-only: it may define fixture
identities, classifications, checksums, metric categories, denominator rules
and thresholds, but it must not create or tune fixture bytes, register a
candidate, run benchmark/held-out evaluation or begin 0.2E.
